Automation is highly critical within managing hundreds of cloud servers, as managing these manually would cause a larger margin for error. Reminder, handling things manually at a scale of hundreds actually isn't scalable. Humans are highly prone to errors compared to machines.

One of the biggest dangers of automation is the fact that automation can cause a massive cascade of failures if not handled with care. Automation is meant to perform the tasks it is given perfectly. This is really dangerous as it is not aware of errors given to it and won't stop unless instructed to stop (essentially, human intervention can only stop it usually).

Bash scripts are really different compared to a docker-compose.yaml file. This is mostly due to how it is structured, how it manages things, and also how it executed. Docker compose files are declarative, no structure other than pointing to preferences and such, it also automatically tracks stuff. Bash though? Procedural and imperative. This is executed line by line and the sequence matters here. It doesn't really track anything unless stated.
